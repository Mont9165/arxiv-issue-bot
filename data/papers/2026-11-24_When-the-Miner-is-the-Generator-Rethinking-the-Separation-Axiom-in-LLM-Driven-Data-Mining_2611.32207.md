# When the Miner is the Generator: Rethinking the Separation Axiom in LLM-Driven Data Mining

- arXiv ID: `2611.32207v1`
- Authors: Xi Chen, Mengke Li
- Published: 2026-11-24
- Primary category: `cs.DB`
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32207v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32207v1)

## Abstract

The theoretical foundation of data mining implicitly relies on an unstated premise, the Separation Axiom (SA), which assumes that mining tools and the data being analyzed are epistemologically independent. However, this premise is increasingly challenged by the emergence of LLM-driven AI agents that actively participate in data generation. We argue that this shift leads to a new epistemic failure mode, which we term ontological self-reference, where discovered patterns may reflect the inductive biases of the tools rather than the underlying structure of the data. This raises fundamental concerns about the credibility of knowledge discovery and the risk of self-reinforcing epistemic loops, where inferred patterns may reflect model-internal biases rather than the underlying structure of the data. We therefore call for a rethinking of the data mining paradigm in the era of large language models, grounded in an SA-aware theoretical foundation and extended to system-level epistemic safeguards. We argue that such a shift is necessary to conceptually rearm the discipline, preventing future knowledge discovery from collapsing into self-justifying algorithmic echo chambers.
