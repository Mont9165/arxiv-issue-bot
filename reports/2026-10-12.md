### 1. Same Query, Different Answer: Run-to-Run Variance in Semantic Query Processing

- arXiv ID: `2610.13823v1`
- Authors: Oktie Hassanzadeh, Dharmashankar Subramanian
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13823v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13823v1)

Semantic query engines ask a large language model (LLM) to judge individual rows, and benchmarks compare these engines on the quality of a few executions at temperature 0. We measure how much another execution changes. We re-execute the text and tabular workloads of SemBench, LRO-Bench, and UDA-Bench through their own harnesses, log every one of 1.2 million model calls, and reanalyze SemBench's published executions. At temperature 0, between 0.07% and 10.2% of rows change their operator output over five executions, depending on the model, and up to 24.0% on one task. Plans amplify these changes exponentially in the number of rows an answer exposes, as a possible-worlds model predicts: on UDA-Bench, a few percent of extracted values change, but about two thirds of the answers do. In SemBench's published results, 21% of text results change quality between executions, including on engines whose plans are deterministic. Several per-query system comparisons, and 16 of 28 tests of LRO-Bench's implementation recommendations, are not settled by the executions the benchmarks run. Changing rows are rows on which models disagree with each other; identical requests within one execution disagree as often as across executions; and one execution's decision margin or reasoning length predicts which rows change. Voting reduces the variance but does not remove it, and a calibrated cascade keeps its guarantee while the rows it returns change. We derive reporting rules for benchmarks and design rules for engines.

### 2. MatQuery: Execution-Grounded Natural-Language Querying over Heterogeneous Materials Databases

- arXiv ID: `2610.13573v1`
- Authors: Chen Jason Zhang, Qi Chen
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13573v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13573v1)

Materials scientists increasingly combine information from several public databases, each with its own interface, schema, units and calculation conventions. MatQuery is a prototype and experimental infrastructure for studying how a natural-language request can be turned into queries over such databases and how the returned values can be interpreted. This report describes what has been built and the preliminary results obtained so far. The prototype holds frozen, self-verifying local copies of six public materials databases (about 10,000 records each), a catalog of declared fields derived from them, a database-independent query representation with an adapter for each database, and an eight-step pipeline in which a language model proposes and deterministic program steps check, execute and assemble. A benchmark generator produces questions whose reference queries are verified by execution on the frozen copies and whose wording is checked by back-translation and review. An evaluation harness runs direct prompting, CHESS and Alpha-SQL on the same frozen basis. Preliminary experiments on an 800-question development set show that direct generation with three demonstrations reaches 82.4% strict execution accuracy (56.5% without demonstrations), that a low-cost scorer selects the exact set of databases for 85.0% of complete requests with well-calibrated probabilities, and that band gaps differ by more than 0.5 eV in 31.6% of composition-matched cross-database clusters. Two pilots show the limits of the current approach: a probe-then-ask policy gave answers rated valid by both reviewers in only 11 of 50 sessions, and only 115 of 300 sampled record pairs could be compared by structure. The complete pipeline is in smoke testing and has not yet been evaluated at scale.

### 3. Leakage-Resistant Evaluation of AI-Generated Database Specifications in Specification-Driven Migration

- arXiv ID: `2610.12778v1`
- Authors: Oleg Grynets, Ivan Fedorchuk, Vasyl Lyashkevych
- Published: 2026-10-08
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.DB`, `cs.LO`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12778v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12778v1)

This paper presents a leakage-resistant framework and benchmark (LRFB) for measuring evaluators of AI-generated Oracle PL/SQL specifications. The benchmark contains 494 labelled source-specification pairs across 19 quality metrics grouped into seven dimensions; each metric contains 26 records divided into development and held-out subsets of 13 records each, with three clean and ten defective specifications per subset. Ground truth is mechanically sealed from inference and checked with canary and label-permutation tests. Three LLM judges were evaluated with three runs per record and compared against two trivial baselines. On 247 held-out records, GPT-4o, DeepSeek V4 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.5 reached balanced accuracies of 0.87, 0.89, and 0.81, respectively, while a specification-length baseline reached 0.88. Exact paired tests did not separate any judge from that baseline. The judges nevertheless provided information unavailable to length: the labelled defect category appeared among findings in 84%-92% of violated records, and quoted evidence overlapped the labelled span in 92%-93%. The study therefore reframes specification evaluation from binary issue detection toward evidence-linked, typed findings and benchmark-validity controls.

### 4. Semantic Views: Certified Reuse of LLM Labels Across Semantic Queries

- arXiv ID: `2610.12768v1`
- Authors: Oktie Hassanzadeh, Dharmashankar Subramanian
- Published: 2026-10-08
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12768v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12768v1)

Semantic query engines evaluate natural-language filters with a large language model, and every evaluation is a costly oracle call. Optimizers that reduce these calls face a dilemma: cascades that trust a cheap proxy miss their precision and recall targets on many queries, methods with finite-sample guarantees save little, and all of them discard the oracle's labels when the query ends. We present SemViews, a certified semantic filter that orders tuples by signals that cost no oracle calls (the labels that earlier queries left behind as semantic views, a local embedding, and a helper model's score), refines that order with labels it must pay for anyway, and certifies its answer with fresh stratified oracle samples. The answer meets user precision and recall targets with probability at least $1-δ$ for every workload and every catalog, so a stale or misleading view can cost oracle calls but never accuracy. On four workloads replayed against 725,000 recorded oracle labels, SemViews meets both targets on 99.63% of queries and saves 28%-45% of the oracle calls, whereas the only prior method in our study with the same guarantee needs 1.32x-1.71x as many calls. When a question returns in different words, views cut its calls by a further 27%-36%, with both targets met on at least 99.8% of such queries; similarity caches and entailment-based reuse meet them on only 17%-47%.

### 5. Returning to Development States: When Full Sandbox Snapshots Help Coding Agents

- arXiv ID: `2610.13841v1`
- Authors: Daniel Thi Graviet, Tanmay Shah
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.LG`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13841v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13841v1)

Coding agents often make useful progress before they fail, but retry-from-scratch discards the machine state created along the way. This makes failure recovery an interesting search problem: if intermediate environment states preserve meaningful progress, restoring and branching from them could outperform repeatedly restarting from the initial state. We implement Go-Explore-style search over full Daytona snapshots and find that higher and more efficient solve rates depend mainly on progress that sits outside the repository, such as databases, services, and other runtime state. Our results suggest that full-snapshot search is most effective when long-horizon coding work spans multiple services whose state cannot be reconstructed from a single diff.

### 6. SWE-DeceptionBench: Benchmarking Deceptive Behaviors of LLMs Across the Software Development Lifecycle

- arXiv ID: `2610.13626v1`
- Authors: Xiting Liu, Lin Yang, Yitong Zhang, Jia Li, Shi-Min Hu
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13626v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13626v1)

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used throughout the software development lifecycle. This growing reliance on LLMs raises concerns about their trustworthiness, with deception posing a particularly important risk. Deceptive behavior takes the form of misleading responses or assistance in fabricating, concealing, or distorting information. To study this behavior, we introduce SWE-DeceptionBench, a benchmark for evaluating LLM deception in software engineering. The benchmark covers six lifecycle stages and two deception triggers: Active Deception, where models recognize infeasible or conflicting requirements but still present them as achievable, and Passive Deception, where models help fulfill explicit user requests to mislead others. We further introduce Neutral, Reward, and Pressure conditions to study the effect of external inducements, resulting in 720 test instances in Chinese and English. Evaluating seven mainstream LLMs, we find substantial deception even under the Neutral condition, with aggregate rates of 49.88% for Active Deception and 64.29% for Passive Deception. Deception rates vary across lifecycle stages, and external inducements affect Active and Passive Deception to different extents. We also evaluate lightweight defenses, finding that guard models miss many deceptive requests, and Self-Reminder reduces but does not eliminate deceptive responses. These findings highlight the need for more effective deception detection and mitigation methods to support trustworthy LLM-based software engineering.

### 7. REINS: Agent-guided Thread Scheduling for Concurrency Bug Detection and Reproduction

- arXiv ID: `2610.13421v1`
- Authors: Yu Zhang, Corina S. Pasareanu, Guowei Yang
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13421v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13421v1)

Concurrency bugs manifest only under specific thread interleavings, making them hard to detect and reproduce. Existing controlled concurrency testing techniques take over thread scheduling to explore the interleaving space. As the space grows rapidly with program scale, finding interleavings that can trigger bugs within a limited budget remains challenging. The program's concurrency semantics can reveal which variable accesses and synchronization operations under specific interleavings lead to unexpected program behavior, and thus help focus the exploration on bug-relevant interleavings. However, prior techniques have not fully exploited such semantic information. In this paper, we present REINS, an agentic approach for detecting and reproducing concurrency bugs. At its core, REINS guides thread scheduling with the agent's reasoning about the concurrency semantics and known concurrency bug patterns. REINS starts with runtime observations and combines them with static information to reason about the program's concurrency behavior and formulate hypotheses about potential bugs. A hypothesis translates into a set of constraints on event orders and variable states, guiding a controlled scheduler toward interleavings likely to trigger the bug. REINS draws conclusions from the execution results and keeps refining the constraints until the bug is triggered or the hypothesis is refuted. Compared with existing techniques on three public benchmarks, REINS detects more bugs within the same time budget. Furthermore, REINS found six previously unknown concurrency bugs in real-world projects, three of which have been confirmed by the developers.

### 8. Agent4Spec: An LLM-based Agentic System to Optimize API Specifications through Automated Validation and Market Analysis

- arXiv ID: `2610.13273v1`
- Authors: Yongjian Tang, Costel Moraru, Doruk Tuncel, Hanya Elhashemy, Steffen Klepke, Thomas Runkler
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13273v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13273v1)

API specifications serve as critical documentation for modern software development, directly impacting integration efficiency and client code generation. While recent advancements have leveraged Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate initial specifications from source code, existing approaches treat this process as a one-time event - overlooking the needs for continuous validation, long-term maintenance, and market-driven evolution of these artifacts. To address this issue, we present Agent4Spec, an autonomous multi-agent framework featuring two distinct pipelines: (1) a validation pipeline that detects and self-corrects OpenAPI syntax errors, and (2) a market analysis pipeline that conducts competitive research, identifies functional gaps against industry leaders, and automatically generates missing API-spec functionalities. We evaluate Agent4Spec on two curated datasets of API specifications spanning both general-purpose and industrial scenarios. Among 9 SOTA LLMs, the top-performer achieves 100% syntax error detection rates and over 95% implementation success for proposed corrections, while securing over 50% adoption rates for suggested endpoint improvements, representing a practical tool for API development.

### 9. Understanding Verification Vulnerabilities in Cross-chain Bridges: An Empirical Study

- arXiv ID: `2610.13170v1`
- Authors: Yuying Wang, Yi Guo, Jiachi Chen, Hanwen Zhang, Yi Sun
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13170v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13170v1)

With the rapid development of blockchain technology, numerous heterogeneous blockchains have emerged, each featuring distinct architectures, consensus mechanisms, and application ecosystems. Cross-chain bridges have consequently become a critical infrastructure component, typically relying on smart contracts to facilitate message passing and asset transfers across heterogeneous blockchains. Unlike single-chain contracts, bridge contracts introduce additional verification logic to validate external-chain data, such as state proofs, message sender identities, and signatures. However, correctly implementing such logic is challenging. We refer to vulnerabilities that violate the security guarantees of cross-chain verification as Cross-chain Verification Vulnerabilities (CVVs). To characterize CVVs, we conduct the first systematic empirical study of this vulnerability class. By analyzing 588 security audit reports from 36 real-world cross-chain bridge projects, we derive 11 CVV patterns and organize them into 5 high-level categories according to the affected verification properties. Following this taxonomy, we construct a CVV dataset containing 830 vulnerability instances across 786 contract files. The dataset establishes mappings among audit reports, audited source-code versions, affected source files, and CVV-pattern labels. We then assess existing detection approaches. Current tools provide limited documented coverage of CVV patterns, while only two of four evaluated LLMs outperform an all-pattern constant baseline of 29.3% Macro-F1; the best reaches 51.2%. Together, the limited tool coverage and inconsistent LLM gains motivate detection methods tailored to cross-chain verification semantics.

### 10. SCCGBench: Executability-Oriented Evaluation for Real-World Web Service-Call Code Generation

- arXiv ID: `2610.12920v1`
- Authors: Shuo Wang, Guodong Fan, Ming Zhu, Jing Li, Chun Wang
- Published: 2026-10-09
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12920v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12920v1)

LLM-generated service-call code is increasingly important for service computing, where developers rely on large language models (LLMs) to compose services and invoke third-party services. However, current approaches have not yet evaluated the invocation and execution of services in the actual code environment. To support execution-grounded evaluation, we present the Service-Call Code Generation Benchmark (SCCGBench), a benchmark of 3,135 traceable and decontamination-filtered RapidAPI call functions from real GitHub repositories. SCCGBench uses redacted-intent generation to reduce circular leakage and introduces Mock, Robust, ParamF1, and host-, endpoint-, method-, and parameter-level diagnostics. Mock denotes a virtual-execution hit that simultaneously matches the gold host, endpoint, and HTTP method, while Robust further requires status checking and exception handling. We evaluate six code LLMs and one non-neural retrieval baseline under intent-only, retrieval-augmented, and gold-documentation Oracle settings. Results show that models often generate syntactically correct or superficially similar HTTP-request code but achieve low Mock success in the intent-only setting; the strongest evaluated model obtains only 3.76% Mock. Under the gold-documentation Oracle settings (C3 and C5), Mock reaches 88.73%--90.52% for the open-source models evaluated in RQ2 and 76.47%--81.86% for DeepSeek-V4-Pro, whereas sparse and dense retrieval remain far below this oracle level. The findings indicate that service/API grounding is the main bottleneck: Mock is dominated by host, endpoint, and method alignment, while ParamF1 separately reveals parameter-alignment errors. SCCGBench provides an execution-grounded benchmark for Web service invocation, API recommendation, service-interface retrieval, endpoint grounding, and reliable service-call automation.

### 11. Meta-Reflective Evolution Effectively Improves Production AI Systems

- arXiv ID: `2610.12794v1`
- Authors: Seung Won Wilson Yoo, Chang Zeng, Aditi Somayajula, Sahithi Ankireddy, Rohan Kosalge, Avishree Khare, John Mitchell, Aditya G. Parameswaran, Rajhans Samdani, Anupam Datta
- Published: 2026-10-08
- Categories: `cs.LG`, `cs.AI`, `cs.CL`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12794v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12794v1)

Large Language Model (LLM)-guided evolutionary optimization has successfully improved prompts, programs, and even entire AI systems. Most optimization methods select a candidate from the search frontier, then mutate it in one LLM pass based only on its failures. This focus on the frontier can stall on production AI systems, where isolated failures reveal little about complex, hidden interactions. We introduce CoCoEvolve (Cortex Code Evolve), an evolutionary optimizer built around what we call meta-reflection, wherein an agentic mutator (Cortex Code) selectively retrieves evidence from the entire evolutionary history and probes the live system before verifying and proposing mutations. We apply CoCoEvolve to optimize three classes of production AI systems: data agents, LLM-powered data pipelines, and data engineering workflows. On DABStep, CoCoEvolve tops the public leaderboard, reaching 92.8% held-out hard accuracy compared to 78.4% for Meta-Harness and 30.4% for GEPA under matched conditions. Across six LLM data pipeline workloads, it outperforms every evaluated baseline. On data-eng-bench, CoCoEvolve lifts test-set solve rate from 59.1% to 77.4%, past the best baseline of 73.6%. It also has 11 percentage points higher accuracy than GEPA on HotpotQA while remaining competitive with specialized optimizers on established mathematical and systems benchmarks. Matched ablations confirm that each component of CoCoEvolve contributes to these gains, demonstrating the value of meta-reflection for optimizing production AI systems.

### 12. Quantifying SMT Solver Instability for Formal Verification

- arXiv ID: `2610.12649v1`
- Authors: Daniel Evans, Rylie Hansen, John Shorter
- Published: 2026-10-08
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12649v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12649v1)

Program verification has become commonplace in software development projects, and much of its feasibility can be attributed to the potential of SMT solvers to automate model checking and other verification techniques. However, SMT instability, a phenomenon where semantically equivalent permutations of source code take much longer to solve or timeout, has been found to interrupt workflows and make software engineers more likely to avoid verification. Previous efforts to measure SMT instability have had difficulty quantifying it due to the abnormal runtime distributions it produces. In this paper, we use the coefficient of variation to model instability across an extensive dataset of SMT queries from three software domains. Our results show that SMT instability is significant in each area, and that it increases for queries that take longer on average. However, using a novel method to quantify instability, we show that a different approach is possible: if a solution were to optimize the structure of queries for runtime, rather than variance, instability could be mitigated while also improving overall solver performance.

### 13. Fix What's Wrong, Keep What's Right: Hallucination Repair for LLM-Based Optimization Modeling

- arXiv ID: `2610.12529v1`
- Authors: Jiageng Wu, Chao Shen, Zihan Guo, Junyan Shao, Zongyan Zhang, Jie Song, Zhong Li, Mingyang Sun
- Published: 2026-10-08
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `math.OC`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12529v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12529v1)

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being explored to automate operations research modeling, reduce modeling time and lower the expertise barrier to optimization based decision support. However, this process often introduces inconsistencies between generated artifacts and the original problem, which we refer to as optimization modeling hallucinations. Despite progress in general-purpose hallucination mitigation, optimization modeling poses a distinct repair challenge: restoring fidelity to the original problem through coordinated model-code updates. We introduce OPT-Kraken, a training-free hallucination repair framework. To the best of our knowledge, it is the first post-hoc framework for repairing hallucinations in LLM-based optimization modeling artifacts. It extracts problem requirements and instance data, localizes errors by checking problem-model fidelity, model-code consistency, and data grounding, and selects repair granularity by the affected scope. For local repair, category rules and instance evidence are compiled into contracts specifying permitted edits, expected effects, and protected content. Experiments on established optimization modeling benchmarks show positive gains across automatic modeling frameworks and fine-tuned models. On zero-shot artifacts from multiple base models, OPT-Kraken effectively repairs optimization modeling hallucinations while preserving initially correct artifacts. These results demonstrate effective and controlled repair for LLM-based optimization modeling artifacts.
